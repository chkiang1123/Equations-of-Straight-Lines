Slope and inclination

Slope is a number, but it is also an angle. Connecting the two makes both ideas easier to picture — and it explains why a vertical line has no slope at all.

Compare with what I thought

A bigger angle near 90° should mean a steeper road. If 45° is exactly halfway between flat and upright, its slope ought to be 1 (rise equals run). And once the angle passes 90°, the road is going down to the right — so its slope must become negative. The angle and the slope are two views of the same tilt.

3The mathematics

An angle instead of a number

Definition of inclination

The inclination θ of a line is the angle measured anticlockwise from the positive direction of the x-axis to the line:

0° ≤ θ < 180°

Note the strict upper bound: an angle of 180° would point along the positive x-axis again, which is the same direction as 0°, so we stop just short of it.

Why m = tan θ

Look at a line through the origin making angle θ with the x-axis. Walk a distance of 1 along the x-direction; the vertical climb is exactly the tangent of the angle.

  1. Take any point on the line at run = 1 to the right. Its height is tan θ by the right-triangle definition.
  2. But slope means rise / run. With run = 1, slope = tan θ / 1.
  3. So the slope of the line is simply the tangent of its inclination.

rise / run = tan θ / 1 = tan θ.

m = tan θ

The four cases

  • θ = 0° ⇒ the line is horizontal, m = 0.
  • 0° < θ < 90° ⇒ the line rises to the right, m > 0.
  • θ = 90° ⇒ tan 90° does not exist, so the line is vertical and the slope does not exist.
  • 90° < θ < 180° ⇒ the line falls to the right, m < 0.
"Does not exist", not "infinity"

tan 90° is not a number — you cannot divide by zero. So a vertical line has no slope, not an "infinite" one. This is the same fact you met on the horizontal-and-vertical page.

Going backwards: θ = tan⁻¹(m)

Given a slope, the inclination is the inverse tangent — but a calculator returns an angle between −90° and 90°. For a negative slope that answer is negative, which is not a valid inclination. The fix:

θ = tan⁻¹(m),   then add 180° if m < 0

This lands the answer safely in the interval [0°, 180°). For example, m = −1 gives tan⁻¹(−1) = −45°, and adding 180° yields 135°.

Parallel and perpendicular, in this language

Two lines are parallel exactly when their inclinations are equal (they point the same way). They are perpendicular when their inclinations differ by 90°. From m = tan θ it follows that perpendicular lines satisfy m₁m₂ = −1 — the rule you already know, now seen as a statement about angles.

Roads are percentages

A road’s steepness is often given as a percentage gradient, which is just 100 × m. A slope of 0.12 is a 12% hill. So a 100% gradient is a slope of 1 — a 45° road.

Slope is the tangent of the angle

A line’s slope is the tangent of the angle it makes with the positive x-axis: m = tan θ. Horizontal means θ = 0°; vertical means θ = 90° and no slope; past 90° the slope goes negative.

5Worked examples

Doing it properly, on paper

Try each one yourself first — write it down. Only then open the solution.

Example 1 — slope of a line with inclination 60°

m = tan 60° = √3 ≈ 1.73.

The line is steep and rising, as expected for an angle close to 90°.

Example 2 — inclination of a line with slope −1

tan⁻¹(−1) = −45°. Since m < 0, add 180°:

θ = −45° + 180° = 135°

Check: tan 135° = −1. Correct, and 135° is in [0°, 180°).

Example 3 — inclination of y = (1/√3)x

The slope is m = 1/√3. We recognise tan 30° = 1/√3, so

θ = tan⁻¹(1/√3) = 30°

(No adjustment needed because m > 0.)

Example 4 — inclination of x + y = 0

Rewrite: y = −x, so m = −1. As in Example 2, θ = 135°.

The line falls to the right — consistent with an angle past 90°.

Example 5 — two lines at 40° and 130°

The inclinations differ by 130° − 40° = 90°, so the lines are perpendicular.

Check with slopes: tan 40° × tan 130° ≈ 0.839 × (−1.192) ≈ −1. The product is −1, confirming perpendicularity.

✓ Check yourself before you move on

Four quick questions. Write your answers down first — then open each one.

  1. What is the slope of a line whose inclination is 45°?
    Answer

    m = tan 45° = 1.

  2. A line has slope −√3. What is its inclination?
    Answer

    tan⁻¹(−√3) = −60°. Since m < 0, add 180°: θ = 120°. Check tan 120° = −√3. (120°.)

  3. A line is vertical. What can you say about its inclination and its slope?
    Answer

    Its inclination is 90°. tan 90° does not exist, so its slope does not exist.

  4. Two lines have inclinations 25° and 115°. Are they perpendicular? Why or why not?
    Answer

    115° − 25° = 90°, so the inclinations differ by 90° — yes, they are perpendicular. Equivalently tan 25° × tan 115° ≈ −1.
