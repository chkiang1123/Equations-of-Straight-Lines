You cannot build equations of lines out of nothing. Before this chapter begins, you need four tools you already half-know: how to read coordinates, how to measure the distance and midpoint between two points, what slope means, and how to tell when two lines are parallel or perpendicular. The mountain rescue trail

A rescue team’s map is a coordinate grid. The hut sits at (1, 2) and the injured climber is at (7, 10), where each unit is 100 m. The shortest path between them is a straight segment. The team needs to know three things at a glance: how far it really is, where the half-way staging post should go, and how steep the climb feels.

  • They are joined by one straight segment.

2Think

What does it make you think? What rule might be hiding in what you saw?

Compare with what I thought

If those two legs were a real triangle, Pythagoras would give the length of the hypotenuse: √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 units = 1 km.

The ratio 8 / 6 = 4 / 3 measures how steep the climb is — climb 8 for every 6 you walk across. That ratio is exactly what we will call the slope.

And the half-way post is just the midpoint of the two ends. Since two numbers pin down distance, steepness and the middle, there must be formulas for all three. This page is those formulas.

1The mathematics

Four tools you already own

Tool 1 — reading coordinates and the four quadrants

Every point is written (x, y): how far across (x, right is positive) and how far up (y, up is positive). The two axes split the plane into four quadrants, numbered anticlockwise from the top right:

  • Quadrant I: x > 0, y > 0
  • Quadrant II: x < 0, y > 0
  • Quadrant III: x < 0, y < 0
  • Quadrant IV: x > 0, y < 0

Points on an axis belong to no quadrant. The origin (0, 0) is the meeting point.

Tool 2 — distance

Place the two points A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂). The horizontal gap is x₂ − x₁ and the vertical gap is y₂ − y₁. Those are the two legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the distance AB.

AB = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) 

Square the two gaps, add them, take the square root. No sign survives the squaring, so order does not matter.

The legs are the two gaps; the hypotenuse is the distance.

Why it works

Pythagoras on the triangle gives (distance)² = (run)² + (rise)². Substitute the gaps for the legs and take the root. The rescue trail gave √(6² + 8²) = 10, exactly as you saw.

Tool 3 — midpoint

The half-way point is just the average of the two x’s and the average of the two y’s.

M = (x₁ + x₂,   y₁ + y₂)2

In the rescue case: M = ((1 + 7)/2, (2 + 10)/2) = (4, 6). The staging post sits at (4, 6), 500 m out from each end.

Tool 4 — slope

Slope is the steepness: how much you climb for each step right.

m = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁ = riserun
  • Positive slope: the line rises left-to-right.
  • Negative slope: it falls left-to-right.
  • Zero slope: perfectly flat, y = constant.
  • No slope: a vertical line, where x₂ = x₁ and you would divide by zero.
It does not matter which way you travel

Swap the two points and both top and bottom flip sign, so the fraction is unchanged: m = (y₁ − y₂)/(x₁ − x₂) gives the same value. Always keep the same order on top and bottom, though.

Parallel and perpendicular lines

Two lines with the same slope never meet — they are parallel. Two lines at right angles are perpendicular, and their slopes are negative reciprocals: one is the flip-and-negate of the other.

parallel:  m₁ = m₂     perpendicular:  m₁ · m₂ = −1
Why m₁ · m₂ = −1 ?

Imagine one line of slope m = rise/run. A perpendicular line must climb what the first ran, and run what the first climbed, but backwards — so its slope is −run/rise = −1/m. Multiply them: m · (−1/m) = −1. (A vertical line, with no slope, is perpendicular to a horizontal line, slope 0 — the one case the product cannot show.)

The toolbox in one breath

Distance measures the gap, midpoint finds the middle, slope measures steepness, and comparing slopes tells you parallel (m₁ = m₂) or perpendicular (m₁m₂ = −1). Everything in this course is built from these four.

3Worked examples

Doing it properly, on paper

Try each one yourself first — write it down. Only then open the solution.

Example 1 — distance, slope and midpoint of A(1, 2) and B(7, 10)

Distance. Δx = 7 − 1 = 6, Δy = 10 − 2 = 8.

AB = √(6² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10

Slope. m = 8 / 6 = 43.

Midpoint. M = ((1 + 7)/2, (2 + 10)/2) = (4, 6).

These are exactly the rescue-trail numbers from the case study.

Example 2 — A(−2, 3), B(4, −1), and the perpendicular bisector

Midpoint: M = ((−2 + 4)/2, (3 + (−1))/2) = (1, 1).

Slope of AB: m = (−1 − 3)/(4 − (−2)) = (−4)/6 = −23.

Perpendicular slope = negative reciprocal = 32.

Through (1, 1):

y − 1 = 32(x − 1)
Example 3 — are these two lines perpendicular?

Line 1 through (0, 0) and (4, 2): m₁ = (2 − 0)/(4 − 0) = 12.

Line 2 through (0, 0) and (−2, 4): m₂ = (4 − 0)/(−2 − 0) = 4−2 = −2.

Product: m₁m₂ = 12 · (−2) = −1. Yes — perpendicular.

Example 4 — A(3, 5), B(3, −2): the no-slope case

Δx = 3 − 3 = 0. The slope formula would have a zero denominator, so the slope does not exist and the line is vertical:

x = 3

The distance is still fine: Δy = −2 − 5 = −7, so AB = 7. Midpoint = (3, 1.5).

Distance and midpoint never break; only slope does.

4Watch out

Five ways to lose marks

MistakeWhat goes wrongDo this instead
m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₁ − x₂)Top and bottom subtracted in different orders — the sign flipsUse the same point as “2” in both: (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
m = −8/6 (for a falling line)Dropped the minus when Δy was negativeKeep the sign: a falling line has a negative slope
perpendicular to slope 3 is −1/3Called the reciprocal “−1/3” without flipping firstFlip and negate: 3 → 1/3 → −1/3; 1/3 → 3 → −3
distance = √((−6)² + …) dropped the squareForgot to square the negative gapThe square removes the sign anyway, so just square the magnitude
slope 0 for a vertical lineThought “no slope” meant “slope 0”Vertical means no slope (undefined); slope 0 means horizontal

✓ Check yourself before you move on

Four quick questions. Write your answers down first — then open each one.

  1. Find the distance between (2, −1) and (5, 3).
    Answer

    Δx = 3, Δy = 4. Distance = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5.

  2. Find the slope of the line through (−3, 4) and (1, −2).
    Answer

    m = (−2 − 4)/(1 − (−3)) = (−6)/4 = −3/2.

  3. Find the midpoint of (6, 8) and (−2, 2).
    Answer

    M = ((6 + (−2))/2, (8 + 2)/2) = (4/2, 10/2) = (2, 5).

  4. A line has slope 2 and another has slope −1/2. Are they parallel, perpendicular, or neither?
    Answer

    Product: 2 · (−1/2) = −1, so they are perpendicular. Slopes are not equal, so not parallel.
