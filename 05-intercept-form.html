The intercept form

Sometimes the most natural way to describe a line is by where it cuts the two axes. If you know those two cuts, you barely have to do any algebra at all.

3The mathematics

From two axis cuts to one formula

An intercept is simply where a line meets an axis. There are two kinds, and finding each one means putting the other variable equal to zero.

Finding the two intercepts

Work on the budget line 2x + 3y = 12.

  1. x-intercept — put y = 0:
    2x + 3(0) = 12  ⇒  2x = 12  ⇒  x = 6
    The cut is the point (6, 0).
  2. y-intercept — put x = 0:
    2(0) + 3y = 12  ⇒  3y = 12  ⇒  y = 4
    The cut is the point (0, 4).

Put the other variable to zero, and the axis cut appears.

The pattern

Take 2x + 3y = 12 and divide everything by 12:

2x12 + 3y12 = 1212  ⇒  x6 + y4 = 1

The two denominators, 6 and 4, are exactly the two intercepts. So a line that cuts the axes at (a, 0) and (0, b) can always be written as:

xa + yb = 1
The intercept form

A line that cuts the x-axis at (a, 0) and the y-axis at (0, b) has equation x/a + y/b = 1. The denominators are the intercepts.

Why this is enough

Substitute the x-intercept (a, 0): aa + 0b = 1 + 0 = 1 — it works. Substitute the y-intercept (0, b): 0 + 1 = 1 — it works too.

Two points fix a unique line, so if both intercepts satisfy the equation, every point on the line does. The form is genuine.

The slope, read off the intercepts

Rearrange x/a + y/b = 1 into slope-intercept form:

yb = 1 − xa  ⇒  y = b − bax  ⇒  y = −bax + b

So the slope is m = −b/a and the y-intercept is b. For the budget line, a = 6, b = 4, giving m = −4/6 = −2/3 — a downward slope, exactly as the picture showed.

A quick sanity check

Because m = −b/a, the slope and the two intercepts are tied together. If you know any two of {a, b, m}, you can find the third. That is why this form is so handy for sketching.

The limits of the form

The intercept form is brilliant, but it cannot describe every line. Three families escape it:

  • A line through the origin cuts both axes at (0, 0), so a = 0 and b = 0. Dividing by zero is impossible.
  • A horizontal line (other than the x-axis) never cuts the x-axis, so it has no x-intercept — that means a does not exist.
  • A vertical line (other than the y-axis) never cuts the y-axis, so it has no y-intercept — that means b does not exist.
Do not force it

These three are still perfectly good lines — they are just written in other forms (y = k, x = h, or y = mx). The intercept form simply is not the right tool for them.

5Worked examples

Doing it properly, on paper

Try each one yourself first — write it down. Only then open the solution.

Example 1 — intercepts 5 and −3

Step 1. x-intercept a = 5, y-intercept b = −3.

x5 + y−3 = 1   or   x5 − y3 = 1

Step 2. Clear denominators by multiplying by 15: 3x − 5y = 15.

3x − 5y = 15

A negative intercept keeps the plus sign in the form; the minus is carried by the denominator.

Example 2 — 2x + 5y = 20, write in intercept form

Step 1. x-intercept: y = 0 ⇒ 2x = 20 ⇒ x = 10. y-intercept: x = 0 ⇒ 5y = 20 ⇒ y = 4.

Step 2. Substitute a = 10, b = 4:

x10 + y4 = 1

Step 3. Slope: m = −b/a = −4/10 = −2/5.

Example 3 — 4x − 3y = 12, write in intercept form

Step 1. x-intercept: y = 0 ⇒ 4x = 12 ⇒ x = 3. y-intercept: x = 0 ⇒ −3y = 12 ⇒ y = −4.

Step 2. Substitute a = 3, b = −4:

x3 + y−4 = 1

Step 3. Slope: m = −b/a = −(−4)/3 = 4/3.

Example 4 — through (0, −6) and (4, 0)

Step 1. The point on the y-axis is (0, −6), so b = −6. The point on the x-axis is (4, 0), so a = 4.

Step 2. Intercept form:

x4 + y−6 = 1

Step 3. General form: multiply by 12 ⇒ 3x − 2y = 12.

3x − 2y = 12
6Watch out

Three ways to lose marks

MistakeWhat goes wrongDo this instead
x/a − y/b = 1 for a negative bThe sign gets flipped twice and the line is wrongKeep the plus: x/a + y/b = 1. When b is negative the denominator carries the minus, so it reads x/a − y/|b| = 1
Using x/a + y/b = 1 on a line through (0,0)a = 0 and b = 0, so you divide by zeroThese lines are written y = mx (or y = 0, x = 0). The intercept form cannot reach them
Treating a and b as lengthsYou drop the minus sign on a negative intercepta and b are directed intercepts — coordinates, so they can be negative. (0, −4) really means −4

✓ Check yourself before you move on

Four quick questions. Write your answers down first — then open each one.

  1. A line cuts the axes at (8, 0) and (0, 2). Write its intercept form and its slope.
    Answer
    x8 + y2 = 1

    Slope m = −b/a = −2/8 = −1/4.

  2. Write 3x + 2y = 18 in intercept form.
    Answer

    x-intercept: 3x = 18 ⇒ x = 6. y-intercept: 2y = 18 ⇒ y = 9.

    x6 + y9 = 1
  3. A line has intercept form x/4 + y/(−5) = 1. Where does it cut the axes, and what is its slope?
    Answer

    By reading the denominators: x-intercept a = 4, so (4, 0); y-intercept b = −5, so (0, −5). Slope m = −b/a = −(−5)/4 = 5/4.

  4. Can x/a + y/b = 1 describe the line y = 7? Explain.
    Answer

    No. The line y = 7 is horizontal and never cuts the x-axis, so it has no x-intercept (a does not exist). It is written directly as y = 7 instead.
