1See

What is actually written there?

Compare with what I noticed
  • Five completely different-looking pieces of algebra.
  • The number 2 appears in every single one of them.
  • The number −4 (or −4 hidden as a denominator) appears in three of them.
  • Only one has y isolated on the left; only one has everything on the left.
  • Two of them are written as fractions.
2Think

What pattern might connect them?

Compare with what I thought

Every one of them is built from the same two facts: the slope is 2, and the line passes through certain fixed points.

Anita and Bilal are using different anchor points. Chen is using the two intercepts. Dana has simplified to y = mx + c. Eli has pushed everything to one side.

So a “different answer” usually just means “a different form of the same answer”. To check, I should be able to convert any one of them into any other.

Spoiler, in one sentence

All five expand to y = 2x − 4. They are the same line written five ways, and every one of them is a correct answer — unless the question specifically asks for a particular form.

The five forms, side by side

Vertical lines are always written directly as x = h, and horizontal lines as y = k.

If you only remember three formulas

slope = rise ÷ run  ·  y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)  ·  m = −A/B

⇆ Conversions

Getting from any form to any other

To slope-intercept — y = mx + c

  1. From point-slope: expand the bracket, then move the constant across.
  2. From two-point: work out the slope first, then treat it as point-slope.
  3. From intercept: multiply out and rearrange — the slope comes out as −b/a and the intercept as b.
  4. From general: By = −Ax − C, so y = −(A/B)x − C/B.

To general — Ax + By + C = 0

  1. From slope-intercept: move everything to one side.
  2. Clear fractions by multiplying through by the denominator.
  3. Prefer A positive — if A is negative, multiply the whole equation by −1.
  4. Cancel any common factor so A, B and C are the smallest possible integers.
The universal move

When in doubt, aim for y = mx + c. Every form except the vertical line can be rearranged into it, and once you are there you can read off the slope and the intercept instantly, and convert onward to anything else.

⚙ Quick facts

The numbers you need on speed dial

dDistance between two points

√((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

Pythagoras on the rise and the run.

MMidpoint

((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

Average each coordinate separately.

mSlope

(y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

Same order on the top and the bottom.

∥Parallel lines

m₁ = m₂

Equal slopes, different intercepts.

⊥Perpendicular lines

m₁ × m₂ = −1

Flip the fraction and change the sign.

IFrom the general form

m = −A/B

x-intercept −C/A, y-intercept −C/B.

θInclination

m = tan θ

0° to 180°, measured anticlockwise from +x. Add 180° when m is negative.

XIntersections

solve both together

Different slopes → one point. Same slope → none. Same line → infinitely many.
